# Scadable: frequently asked questions

## What is Scadable?

Scadable is a search company building the intelligence layer for companies, starting with compliance. Its first product is a compliance engine that fixes what it finds: it identifies what does not meet a standard across a company's systems, fixes it, reports with evidence, and prepares the company for an independent audit.

## What does "the intelligence layer" mean?

It is an index of how a company actually works, its systems, documents and decisions, that answers questions with the source attached. Every answer is correct, complete and cited, or the system says it does not know. Compliance is the first place Scadable applies it.

## Why does a search company start with compliance?

Because an auditor is the strictest reader a search system can have. An auditor checks every claim and asks for the source of every answer. Answers that hold up in an audit hold up anywhere, and compliance is work companies already need done today.

## How is Scadable different from Vanta, Drata or Secureframe?

Those tools connect to your systems, monitor them and flag problems. Scadable fixes what it finds: a pull request you approve, a setting changed with your sign-off, or a guide sent to the person who has to act. The difference is doing the work and standing behind it, not covering more frameworks.

## Does Scadable issue the SOC 2 report?

No. An independent audit firm examines the controls and issues the report. Scadable prepares the evidence, the policies and the answers to the auditor's requests, so the company arrives at the audit ready. Keeping the auditor independent is what makes the report worth having.

## Who is Scadable for?

Software and AI companies whose enterprise customers ask for a SOC 2 report before they sign, and who want the compliance work done rather than handed to their engineers as a list.

## Which systems does Scadable connect to?

Today, GitHub, Google Cloud, Google Workspace, Slack and OpenAI, plus public checks on a company's own domain. More connections are added as customers need them.

## What does "fixes what it finds" look like in practice?

A failing check becomes a concrete change. For code or configuration, that is a pull request the company reviews and merges. For an account setting, a change made with the owner's sign-off. For something only a person can do, a short guide sent to that person, with the steps and the reason.
